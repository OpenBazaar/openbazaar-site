Set up shop on a market nobody owns

OpenBazaar is a free app for buying and selling directly with other people. Your store runs on your own computer, you get paid in Bitcoin, and there’s no company in the middle to take a cut or close your account.

Your shop isn’t someone else’s account

Big marketplaces rent you a spot, take a cut of every sale and can switch you off. On OpenBazaar the store is yours, because it runs on your machine.

Read the design paper →

It runs on your computer

The app is the store. There’s no sign-up with a company, so there’s no account for anyone to suspend and no platform fee on your sales.

Orders go straight to the seller

Listings are public. Your order, shipping address included, is encrypted for the seller alone and sent over Tor, a network built for privacy.

It can stay open all night

The desktop app keeps your store open while it’s running. For a store that never closes, run the home version on a spare always-on computer and manage it from your phone or laptop.

How a trade works

Three steps, and nobody holding your money in between.

YouYour OpenBazaar app
Order, sent privately over Tor
Payment lockedReleased when you both agree
Signed listings and replies
The sellerTheir own OpenBazaar app

Moderator Only for disputes, and can only choose between payouts you both signed in advance

  1. Find a store you trust

    Every store and listing is signed by the person who posted it, so your app can tell if anything was changed along the way. A signature proves who posted it, not that they’re honest, so look at a store’s history of finished trades too.

  2. Send your order straight to them

    Your order goes from your app to theirs, encrypted and routed over Tor. There’s no company server in between storing it or reading it.

  3. Pay in Bitcoin, held safely in between

    With escrow, your payment is locked so neither of you can run off with it. It’s released when you both agree. In a dispute, a moderator can only pick between payouts you both approved up front, and if the seller goes quiet you get a refund after an agreed time.

    Escrow works on the test network only for now. You can also pay a seller directly, without that protection.

Under the hood

For the curious and the skeptical: what the current code actually does, and where its limits are.

Inspect the implementation ↗
Identity and data integritySigned stores and listings+

BIP 340 Schnorr signatures authenticate canonical record bodies. A listing address is the SHA-256 hash of its body, and an order commits to that address. A signature doesn’t guarantee the goods, the seller, or the latest price.

Read the record implementation ↗
Private order transportTor between trading nodes+

Sale endpoints accept loopback HTTP or a v3 onion reached through the node’s Tor client. Clearnet sale hosts and redirects away from the onion are refused. This does not make all node traffic anonymous; chain, rate, and optional service configuration also matter.

Inspect the transport boundary ↗
Bitcoin escrow, testnet4 onlyTwo spend keys and a bounded moderator+

Escrow aggregates only the buyer and seller keys into a 2-of-2 MuSig2 Taproot output. The moderator can release a share for a presigned outcome; it has no spend key in the output and cannot choose an arbitrary payout. The silent case is the buyer’s presigned refund at the agreed height.

Mainnet moderated escrow is refused pending protocol review. Direct pay does not provide escrow protection.

Read the escrow protocol ↗
Discovery, with an explicit trust dependencySigned introductions and direct follows+

Bundled introductions supply directory data verified under a shipped key. That is a discovery trust dependency, not a permissionless DHT. A node can disable introductions and follow a store’s onion address directly; doing so removes directory discovery and announcements.

Inspect directory replication ↗
Self-hostingA home node you can pair with+

An always-on node exposes a restricted Tor admin service to paired devices. Each device has its own key and signs requests; spending also requires the wallet passphrase and a spend ticket. An optional encrypted LAN channel supports local access.

Home-node setup and threat model ↗
Reputation and public proofsA trade count and a time-locked bond+

Finished escrow payouts contribute to a store’s count. A new seller needs a passing bond to start escrow: at least 10,000 sats locked for at least 2,016 blocks. The bond remains spendable only by the store key after its lock; there is no slash key. These are testnet4 mechanisms.

v0.1.0-alpha.3

Get in early

The desktop app runs everything itself: open it and you’re on the network, quit and you’re off. Want your store open around the clock? Run the home node on an always-on computer. Every file comes from the alpha.3 GitHub release, and you can check yours against SHA256SUMS.

Expect a warning on first launch These alpha builds aren’t signed yet. On macOS, open the app once, then go to System Settings → Privacy & Security and choose “Open Anyway” (on older macOS, right-click the app and choose Open). On Windows, choose “More info” then “Run anyway” on the SmartScreen prompt. On Linux, run chmod +x on the AppImage first.
