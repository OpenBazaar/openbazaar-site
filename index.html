Get the app

Your own shop. No fees. No one to shut it down.

OpenBazaar is a free app for selling from your own storefront and buying straight from independent sellers. Pay with Bitcoin, and with escrow your payment is held until the order arrives.

For sellers

Sell without a platform in the middle

Big marketplaces take a cut of every sale, can hold your payouts, and can close your shop after a policy change. Here there’s no company account and nobody taking a percentage.

No fees on your sales

No listing fees, no percentage, no monthly plan. Just the normal network fee when coins move.

No account to suspend

Your shop belongs to recovery words only you hold, so there’s no review team that can freeze it overnight.

Paid straight to your wallet

Buyers pay into the wallet built into your app. No platform balance, no payout schedule, no reserve.

A storefront that’s yours

Your name, logo, banner and policies, without building or hosting a website.

Your shop can

  • Sell physical goods or downloads
  • Show up to 8 photos per listing
  • Offer sizes, colors and other options
  • Price in dollars, euros, pounds or yen
  • Set shipping prices and where you ship
  • Run coupon codes
  • Add tracking numbers
  • Reply to verified reviews

For buyers

Your money waits until it arrives

You buy straight from the seller, and the whole price goes to them. Your protection comes from how the payment works, not from a company deciding to take your side.

  1. Order

    Check out with your address. It’s sealed so only that seller can read it, and no platform keeps your history.

  2. Pay

    Pay with Bitcoin and choose escrow. The payment is locked, so neither side can take it.

  3. Ship

    The seller ships and adds the carrier and tracking number to your order.

  4. Confirm

    Confirm it arrived to pay the seller. Then leave a review, which only buyers can write.

If something goes wrong

It never arrives, or isn’t as described
Open a dispute and pay the moderator’s filing fee, which is paid separately and never comes out of the escrow. The moderator you picked at checkout then decides between the two outcomes agreed up front: your money back, or paid to the seller.
The seller goes silent
If they never ship and nobody acts, your payment comes back to you after the time agreed at checkout.
The buyer never confirms
Sellers can open a dispute too, so a buyer who has the goods can’t simply sit on the money.

The moderator can’t take the money, change the amounts or pay anyone else, and their fee is set in advance. Escrow works on the test network today and stays off for real bitcoin until it has had an outside review. Paying a seller directly is quicker, but has none of this protection.

Ways to pay

What you can pay with

Sellers can show prices in dollars, euros, pounds or yen. That’s the price tag. The buyer pays the matching amount in a coin the shop accepts, worked out when the order is placed. Wallet balances can show the same currencies beside Bitcoin, Lightning and Monero, labelled “at mainnet price · test coins, no value”.

Only Bitcoin payments can use escrow. With any other coin you pay the seller directly, without that protection. There’s no card, PayPal or bank transfer. These are test coins only.

  • Bitcoin

    In the app

    Pay from the wallet built into the app or from any Bitcoin wallet. It’s the only coin that can use escrow. The first scan can take a while.

    Bitcoin testnet4 · Escrow available

  • Zcash

    In the app, experimental

    A privacy-focused coin. Shops can switch it on, and buyers pay the order’s QR code from any Zcash wallet. The app can pay an order from its own wallet too. Sending Zcash to an address you choose isn’t in yet.

    Zcash test network · No escrow · Direct pay

  • Monero

    In the app

    A privacy-focused coin, on in the desktop app and the home node. The app connects through Tor on its own, so you don’t set up a Monero server or install Tor. There’s no escrow, and the first sync can take a while. Incoming Monero shows up as unconfirmed right away, and only counts toward your balance or an order once it confirms.

    Monero test network · No escrow

  • Lightning

    In the app

    On in the desktop app and the home node. It uses Mutinynet, a test network separate from this app’s Bitcoin. There’s no escrow. The app opens a channel when someone pays.

    Mutinynet · Not Bitcoin testnet4 · No escrow

Honest answers

What you’d want to know before you install anything.

Can I use it for real sales today?

No. This is an early test version (alpha 10) that runs on test networks, where coins are free and worth nothing. Set up a shop, place test orders and tell us what’s missing, but don’t send it real money. The apps aren’t signed with a developer certificate, so macOS may say the app is damaged; the download section explains how to open it.

Who else is on it?

Very few people so far: three demo shops so you can try a whole order, plus shops opened by other early testers. Open one now and you’re among the first, and what you ask for shapes what gets built.

How will buyers find my shop?

Buyers browse and search the marketplace inside the app, and can follow a shop directly. There are no ads or promoted listings, so early on your best customers are the ones you bring.

Does my computer have to stay on?

Your shop takes orders while the app is running. To stay open around the clock, run the home version on a spare always-on computer and manage it from your laptop or Android phone.

Can buyers and sellers message each other?

Yes. Buyers and sellers can write to each other inside an order. The seller can also add a note when they mark an order shipped.

What if I lose my computer?

Your shop and wallet come from a set of recovery words, not a company account. Write them down and keep them safe. Nobody can reset them for you.

Is there a phone app?

There’s an Android app for managing a shop that runs on a home computer, installed from the file below rather than Google Play. There’s no iPhone app yet.

Can I send coins to another wallet?

Yes. In the wallet, Send moves Bitcoin or Monero to an address you paste. Lightning has Pay invoice for an invoice you paste, and Withdraw for its on-chain balance, which is Mutinynet and separate from the Bitcoin wallet. Zcash can pay an order from the app; sending it to an address you choose isn’t in yet. A send asks for your wallet password again. These are still test coins.

Version 0.1.0 alpha 10

Try it today, with test coins

One app does everything, with no sign-up. Open it and your shop is open, quit and it closes. The app connects through Tor on its own; you don’t install Tor.

Open a shop

  1. Install the app and set a wallet password.
  2. Choose “Sell” and add your shop’s name, logo and policies.
  3. List something with photos and a price.

Try buying

  1. Install the app, set a wallet password and choose “Shop”.
  2. Get free test coins from a testnet4 faucet and send them to the app’s wallet.
  3. Place a test order from one of the demo shops.

Your computer will warn you the first time These builds aren’t signed with a developer certificate. On macOS, if the dialog says the app is damaged and offers no way to open it (usual on Apple silicon), the app is not damaged. Open Terminal and run xattr -cr /Applications/OpenBazaar.app, then open the app normally. If the dialog instead names an unidentified developer, open the app once, then go to System Settings → Privacy & Security and choose “Open Anyway” (on older macOS, right-click the app and choose Open). On Windows, choose “More info”, then “Run anyway”. On Linux, make the AppImage executable with chmod +x first.
