OpenBazaar: Signed Catalogs, Sealed Orders, and Scriptless Two-Party Escrow for a Non-Custodial Marketplace

Brian Hoffman · OpenBazaar project

Download PDF

Abstract

OpenBazaar is a peer-to-peer marketplace for ordinary physical and digital goods paid in bitcoin. The original system (developed from 2014, with its last substantial releases in 2020) published stores over IPFS, carried orders over libp2p, and protected buyers with a 2-of-3 multisignature escrow whose script and moderator key appeared on chain when it was spent. This paper presents a redesign of the network and argues which of its security and privacy properties hold, and under which assumptions.

Stores and listings are public, content-addressed records signed with BIP 340 Schnorr signatures over a canonical encoding, hardened against control and bidirectional-override characters on both signing and verification, and grouped under a closed 15-category signed taxonomy. Each store signs a catalog index (OBCX) naming its current listing hashes, and the directory signs an admission snapshot (OBAX) beside its fee-scheduled head (OBSD), while every node still verifies each store's on-chain admission fee independently. Listings carry variants, stock, shipping services, coupons, structured product codes, return policies, and fiat prices that both nodes compute identically; buyers inspect verified, receipt-locked reviews before purchase and can open signed post-purchase return cases over the order channel without coupling returns to escrow. Orders are private letters sealed with HPKE to a single vendor and carried over Tor to the onion address in its signed store record. Public web pages served by seller nodes and a public market page at / allow read-only browsing directly from seller onions in Tor Browser, while checkout and key derivation stay out of seller-served JavaScript; we also specify a planned buyer-only light client extension for signed desktop and mobile apps so buyers need not run an always-on node. Payments go directly to the vendor, on chain (with replace-by-fee), over Lightning, or in Monero or Zcash, one transaction per seller. Moderated purchases use a Bitcoin-only scriptless escrow: a MuSig2 Taproot key-path output whose timeout and dispute spends are all presigned before funding. A dispute is settled by an adaptor signature whose completing secret is split between the favored party and a moderator half that the losing party verifiably encrypts to the moderator. The moderator never holds a signing key over the funds and cannot change any payout, and without a release or a ruling the funds return to the buyer. The moderator is paid a filing fee outside the escrow, before it rules, so its pay does not depend on the ruling.

We state the threat model, specify the escrow protocol with the checks each party must run and its block-height timeline, argue the security properties, tabulate what every observer learns, and state the limitations and open problems, including an unenforced moderator filing fee, the size of the verifiable encryption, collusion between the moderator and one party, a single directory, and the remaining prerequisites before mainnet escrow. Measurements of a reference implementation show that catalog signing, verification, and order sealing cost well under a millisecond, and that the escrow's verifiable encryption costs about 50 KB and 50 ms per share.
